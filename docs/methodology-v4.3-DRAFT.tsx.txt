            <div style={ENTRY_EYEBROW}>
              Methodology v4.3 &middot; August 2, 2026
            </div>
            <p style={ENTRY_PARA}>
              Hand-labelled fifty claims against the automated entailment judge, and changed
              the judge based on what that turned up. Raw agreement was 88 percent, Cohen&rsquo;s
              kappa 0.63. The disagreements were not scattered. Nearly all were one case: the
              claim carries a condition qualifier, such as &ldquo;in vulvodynia&rdquo;, that the
              quoted sentence never repeats, because the paper established its population pages
              earlier. The judge had been filling that gap from its own recall of the
              literature, which its instructions forbade and which nothing in the record could
              audit.
            </p>
            <p style={ENTRY_PARA_NEXT}>
              Recomputing those same fifty labels under each reading showed how much rested on
              that one unsettled question. Requiring the condition to appear in the quote gives
              78 percent agreement and kappa 0.44. Allowing it to come from the source gives 94
              percent and kappa 0.79. The measurement was mostly reporting an ambiguity in the
              task rather than the quality of the evidence. (Both of those kappas are computed on
              the enriched validation sample and overstate corpus reliability; see the note on
              design weighting below.)
            </p>
            <p style={ENTRY_PARA_NEXT}>
              The judge now receives the source title and the condition the claim is filed under,
              taken from this database rather than from memory, and may use it for one purpose:
              settling which population a quote concerns. The intervention, the comparator, the
              outcome, and the direction and size of the effect must all appear in the quoted
              passage. Each judgment records whether that context was needed, so the claims
              resting on a filed condition can be listed rather than assumed. At present 218 of
              325 do, which makes correct condition filing load-bearing for two thirds of the
              corpus.
            </p>
            <p style={ENTRY_PARA_NEXT}>
              Quotes stored without their grammatical subject were widened. One read &ldquo;had a
              better safety and tolerability profile than COC&rdquo;, leaving the reader to supply
              the drug it referred to. Scoring also moved to a majority of three passes, because a
              single pass was not reproducible: the judge changes its mind on roughly one
              borderline claim in seven, enough to move the published figure by a point between
              otherwise identical runs. Majority voting stabilises the number. It does not make it
              more correct, and an earlier draft of this entry wrongly implied that it did. Three
              samples drawn from one model are not three opinions; work published in 2026 finds
              that even nine judges drawn from seven different model families supply only about
              two independent votes, because they make the same mistakes on the same items. The
              agreement-with-itself rate is reported here as a variance diagnostic and nothing
              more.
            </p>
            <p style={ENTRY_PARA_NEXT}>
              Entailment across extracted claims now stands at 93.5 percent. That is lower than
              the 97.3 percent reported under v4.2, and the fall is the finding rather than a
              setback: claims asserting a comparison their quote never mentions were downgraded,
              as were claims whose supporting evidence the judge had previously been supplying
              itself. Read that figure against its baseline. A judge that answered
              &ldquo;entailed&rdquo; to every claim without reading anything would score about 95
              percent on this corpus, because the corpus is that skewed. A high entailment rate is
              therefore not by itself evidence of anything, which is the reason for the
              calibration work rather than an argument against it.
            </p>
            <p style={ENTRY_PARA_NEXT}>
              A verification pass over the statistics found two errors in how they had been
              reported here. The validation sample deliberately oversamples the rare neutral
              class, by roughly four times, so that the class the judge struggles with is actually
              represented. That is sound sampling, but Cohen&rsquo;s kappa depends on prevalence as
              well as on rater accuracy, so a kappa computed on an enriched sample overstates
              corpus reliability. Design-weighted back to the corpus distribution, the blind
              round-2 kappa is 0.21 rather than 0.42, with a 95 percent interval from 0.05 to
              0.49. Separately, the rater revised five labels after seeing the machine&rsquo;s
              answers; those revisions are an error analysis and are no longer blind, so the
              figures computed from them are not reliability estimates. The number this project
              stands behind is the blind, design-weighted one.
            </p>
            <p style={ENTRY_PARA_NEXT}>
              The more useful measurement is not a single agreement coefficient at all. Against
              blind human labels the judge recovers 50 percent of the claims a human calls neutral
              (6 of 12, 95 percent interval 25 to 75). Recall on the rare class is what the reader
              of an evidence database actually needs to know, and unlike kappa it does not shift
              when the validation sample is enriched. Two limits remain openly outstanding: the
              labelling has been done by one rater who also built the database, so no
              human-to-human ceiling exists yet, and the &ldquo;contradicted&rdquo; label has never
              once been produced across the whole corpus, which means it is unvalidated and this
              study design cannot detect whether that is correct.
            </p>
